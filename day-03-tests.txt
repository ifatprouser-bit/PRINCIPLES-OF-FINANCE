NOA: A friend owes you money and says, I'll pay you back a bit every month until we're square. Is that an annuity? Take a moment before you answer.

TOM: I want to say yes, but you asked it in a way that makes me nervous.

NOA: Good instinct. A stream has to pass three tests, and it has to pass all three. Same amount. Same gap. And it stops after a known number of payments.

TOM: Your friend fails the first one already. A bit every month isn't the same number every month.

NOA: Now take one that passes. A one year lease. The rent is the same figure each time, the gap is one month every time, and there are twelve of them, then it ends.

TOM: Same shape as a fixed term job. The salary lands on the same date, the same amount, and the contract runs out in eighteen months. You can count them.

NOA: And a gym plan you sign up for and cancel after a year. Same money, same rhythm, known end.

TOM: Rent, salary, gym. Different lives, one shape. So the tests aren't about what the money is for.

NOA: That's the whole point. Now break each test in turn. Rent that goes up after the first year breaks same amount.

TOM: And a month where the tenant pays nothing and catches up later breaks same gap.

NOA: A monthly plan you mean to keep forever breaks the third. It never stops. That one has its own name, a perpetuity, and it comes next.

TOM: So if one test fails, I don't force the formula onto it.

NOA: You split the stream and value each part on its own. Forcing it is where people lose marks.

TOM: Take a breath. One thing to hold?

NOA: Same amount, same gap, and it stops. All three, every time.

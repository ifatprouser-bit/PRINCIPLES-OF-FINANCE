NOA: One question first. You own a car. Your neighbour owes you money. Which of those is closer to owning a share in a company?

NOA: Take a moment. Actually pick one.

TOM: The car. A share feels like a thing you own.

NOA: Most people say that. By tonight you'll say the neighbour.

TOM: So what words am I carrying today?

NOA: Real asset and financial asset. A thing, and a claim on a thing. Then primary market and secondary market, the two places paper changes hands, and only one puts money into a company.

TOM: And equity. I hear it all the time and I've never been sure of it.

NOA: Equity and dividend. Equity is the owners' share of the firm. A dividend is cash the firm pays out to the owners. One is a size, one is a move. Then the three jobs a firm does. Buy assets, raise money, pay cash out. The three shapes a business can take. Sole proprietorship, which is a one-person business. Partnership. Corporation. And three words for how much you really know when you choose. Certainty, uncertainty, ambiguity.

TOM: No maths?

NOA: None today. Today is only about what kind of thing each number is.

NOA: Take a breath and hold one thing. A thing, or a claim on a thing. Everything today hangs off that.

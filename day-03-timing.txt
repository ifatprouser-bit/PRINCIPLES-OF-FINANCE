NOA: You sign a lease today. When does the first rent actually count as paid? Take a moment. Not what feels fair. What the contract says.

TOM: My instinct says today, because today is when something happens.

NOA: That instinct is the most expensive one in this whole topic. Here, unless the question says otherwise, the first payment lands at the end of the first period. Not on the day you sign.

TOM: So the first rent belongs to the end of month one.

NOA: Which means it's one full month away from you. It has to be pulled back one time to reach today. Not zero times. One.

TOM: Give me something I can't argue with.

NOA: Your salary. You work the month first, then you're paid. The month comes before the money.

TOM: The electricity bill too. The meter runs all month, and the bill turns up after the month is gone.

NOA: And someone who works for herself sends the bill after the work is done, never before. Three different lives, one habit. The period happens, then the money moves.

TOM: So on a three year stream, the last payment is three periods away, and it gets pulled back three times.

NOA: The count matches the year number. Every time.

TOM: And if I slip by one, what goes wrong?

NOA: Your answer comes out too big by one period of interest. It looks perfectly fine. Nothing screams. That's exactly why it survives all the way onto the exam paper.

TOM: So the fix isn't being clever. It's asking one boring question first.

NOA: How far away is each payment, counting from today. Answer that before you touch anything else.

TOM: Take a breath. One thing to carry.

NOA: A payment at the end of year one is one year away, so it's discounted once. Never zero.

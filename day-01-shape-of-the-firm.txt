NOA: Two friends run a catering business together, no company paperwork. A big job goes wrong and they owe a supplier far more than the business has in the bank. Whose money pays the rest?

NOA: Take a moment. Be honest about what you expect.

TOM: I want to say the business pays what it can and that's the end of it. But I suspect you'll say their own money.

NOA: Their own savings. That's personal liability, and it comes with the simple shapes. One person alone is a sole proprietorship. Two or more together is a partnership.

TOM: So the business and the owners are one pocket. Then what do they get for carrying that?

NOA: It is simple, and the profit is taxed once. It reaches them, it's taxed, done.

TOM: And a corporation is the opposite bargain. Limited liability. If it goes wrong they take what's inside the company and stop at the door. My savings are on the other side of that door.

NOA: That's the first of three things you buy. The second is a life longer than yours. A one woman repair shop ends the day she stops. A corporation keeps going while owners come and go.

TOM: So the firm lives longer than the people in it. What's the third?

NOA: A stake a stranger can buy. Sell part of a corporation to someone you've never met and the business carries on unchanged. Sell half a partnership to a stranger and the whole thing usually has to be pulled apart and rebuilt.

TOM: So any firm wanting real money from the public has to be a corporation. Strangers only hand over money for something they can sell on again.

NOA: Now the bill. The profit is taxed twice. The company earns it and pays tax. What's left goes out as a dividend, and the shareholder pays tax again on that same money as it lands in her account.

TOM: Two tolls on one road. The partners paid one, the shareholders pay two.

NOA: So in a question, watch for two wants. Outside investors, and nobody paying out of their own pocket. Those two together already decide the form. Then the follow up is what it costs them. Double taxation.

NOA: Take a breath and recall one thing. The corporation is a trade, not a free upgrade. Three things you can't get any other way, paid for with the same money taxed on the way out and again on the way in.

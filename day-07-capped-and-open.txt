NOA: A question before I say anything else. A firm owes its lenders a fixed amount. This year the firm does far better than anyone expected, and the money coming in is double what the lenders were promised. Who gets that extra money? Take a moment and decide.

TOM: The lenders already got what they were promised. So the extra has to go somewhere else.

NOA: It goes to the shareholders. All of it. And this is the part your professor keeps coming back to. Look at the containers again. A bucket has a top edge, so it fills and then it stops. Pour in twice as much water and the senior debt holders still take exactly what they were promised, the face value of their debt. Not one drop more.

TOM: So a good year and an amazing year pay them the same.

NOA: Exactly the same. The junior debt holders too. Their container also has a top edge. But the pool at the bottom has no top edge at all. If the water arrives as a flood, the pool takes the whole flood.

TOM: So an amazing year does nothing for the lenders and everything for the shareholders.

NOA: That's the whole difference between lending to a firm and owning it. A lender can never receive more than the amount promised. A shareholder can receive any amount at all.

TOM: And it must run the other way too.

NOA: It does, and this is the other half. If very little water arrives, the pool is dry. The shareholders get nothing. But nobody can ask them for more, because a pool cannot hold less than nothing.

TOM: So the lenders have a ceiling, and the owners have a floor.

NOA: Both, and that one sentence is most of what this topic is. The lenders are capped at the top edge. The owners can get everything above the promises, or nothing.

NOA: Take a breath and recall one thing. The lenders' container has a top edge, so a huge year pays them the same as a good one. The pool has no top edge, so everything above the promises belongs to the shareholders.

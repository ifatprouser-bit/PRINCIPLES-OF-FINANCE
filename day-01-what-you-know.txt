NOA: Two women open a shop next month. The first has sold her whole stock in advance, agreed price, paid up front. The second opens on a street that didn't exist last year, where nobody has ever traded. Which one can honestly put a number on next month?

NOA: Take a moment. And notice there may be more than two kinds of answer.

TOM: The first can. Her number is signed already. The second cannot. But a third woman is missing.

NOA: Name her.

TOM: Someone in the middle. She has run a cafe on a busy street for four years. She doesn't know next month either, but she isn't guessing blindly. She has her own cash register records.

NOA: Those three are the whole idea. Certainty, uncertainty, ambiguity.

TOM: Certainty is the first shop. The amount is settled before the thing happens. Not probably settled. Signed.

NOA: Uncertainty is your cafe. The outcome is really unknown, but you can put odds on it that you can trust.

TOM: Trust is doing the work there. Her own records, or a model somebody has tested. Not a feeling about how things usually go.

NOA: And ambiguity is the new street. Unknown outcome, and nothing to build honest odds out of. Nobody stops you inventing a number anyway, and that's exactly what the word is there to catch. A guess dressed up as a real number is still a guess.

NOA: Now the trap. A hotel decides whether to heat its pool. The gas is locked by a signed contract, so the cost is settled. Guest numbers aren't, but the hotel holds its own guest records going back years.

TOM: Half fixed, half open. Which label wins?

NOA: Say it first.

TOM: The open half. If one side of the decision is still moving, the decision isn't certain, no matter how solid the other side is.

NOA: The most common mistake in this topic. A locked price next to a quantity nobody can name is still not certainty. That hotel is in uncertainty, because you can put odds you can trust on the open side.

TOM: Take the label from the part you can't pin down.

NOA: One honest note. The course labels the cases with these three names, but it never writes down what they mean. So if the professor words one differently in class, his wording wins.

NOA: Take a breath and recall one thing. Ask whether anyone could honestly put odds on this. If they could, uncertainty. If they could not, ambiguity.

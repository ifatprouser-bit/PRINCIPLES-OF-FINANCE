NOA: Three letters first, because they cost marks. A P R. Take a moment and say out loud what you think they stand for.

TOM: Annual percentage rate. That's the only thing those letters have ever meant to me.

NOA: Everywhere else, yes. Not here. In this course A P R is the Absolute Priority Rule. An order of payment, not a rate.

TOM: So when a question says assume A P R holds, nobody's handed me an interest rate. They've told me the order people get paid in.

NOA: One firm, one pot of money, one date when the debt is due, three claims waiting on it.

TOM: Three claims and one pot. Then the whole day is the order they stand in.

NOA: Senior debt. Borrowed money that has to be repaid first, and the amount promised to that lender on the day is its face value. Then junior debt, also called subordinated debt. Subordinated only means it stands behind. Same kind of promise, later place in the line.

TOM: And the owners come last, which sounds unfair until I remember they're the ones who chose the risk.

NOA: The owners. Equity. Shareholders, stockholders, the same people. Last in line, and they take what's left, which is often nothing. One more word to carry: default. The firm does not pay what it promised on the day it promised.

TOM: Senior, junior, owners. That's the line.

NOA: That's the map. Today is that one sentence, made exact.

NOA: Take a breath and hold one thing. Here A P R is an order, never a rate.

Warm-up

Twenty minutes in the hour before the paper starts. Then close it.

Nothing on this page is new. Every task and every line below comes from days 1 to 9.

The point is not to learn. The point is to get your hands moving on three shapes you already know.

Three tasks to warm the hands

Paper and calculator. Work each one, then open the answer. The numbers are small on purpose.

Task 1. Present value of a mixed stream

You get 1,000 at the end of year 1, then 2,000 at the end of year 2, then 2,000 at the end of year 3. Nothing after that. The required rate of return is 10 percent. What is the stream worth today?

1,000 / 1.10 = 909.09

2,000 / 1.21 = 1,652.89

2,000 / 1.331 = 1,502.63

All three now sit at year 0, so add them: 909.09 + 1,652.89 + 1,502.63 = 4,064.61

The stream is worth 4,064.61 today.

The other route gives the same number. The two 2,000s are an annuity with n = 2 whose first payment is at year 2, so its value lands at year 1: 2,000 × 1.735537 = 3,471.07 at year 1, then 3,471.07 / 1.10 = 3,155.52 at year 0, plus 909.09 = 4,064.61.

Sense check. The cash paid is 5,000. Your answer has to be smaller. It is.

Task 2. The payoff waterfall

At maturity a firm owes 50 to its senior lender and 30 to its junior lender. APR holds. What does each of the three holders get if the assets are worth 110? And if they are worth 65?

The two borders first: FS = 50  ·  FS + FJ = 80

Assets worth 110. 110 is above 80, so both debts are paid in full. Senior 50, junior 30, equity Max(110 − 80, 0) = 30. Check: 50 + 30 + 30 = 110.

Assets worth 65. 65 is above 50 but below 80, so the senior lender is full and the junior lender is short. Senior 50, junior Min(65 − 50, 30) = 15, equity Max(65 − 80, 0) = 0. Check: 50 + 15 + 0 = 65.

Task 3. NPV

A project costs 7,000 at year 0. It brings in 3,000 at the end of year 1, 4,000 at the end of year 2 and 3,000 at the end of year 3. The discount rate is 10 percent. What is the NPV, and what is the decision?

3,000 / 1.10 = 2,727.27

4,000 / 1.21 = 3,305.79

3,000 / 1.331 = 2,253.94

2,727.27 + 3,305.79 + 2,253.94 = 8,287.00

NPV = 8,287.00 − 7,000 = 1,287.00

NPV is 1,287.00. It is above zero, so take the project.

Sense check. The cash coming in is 10,000, so the gain before any discounting is 3,000. The NPV has to be below that. It is.

The trap list

The mistakes this course punishes. Read the list once, slowly.

Watch forThe line
APRIn this course APR means Absolute Priority Rule. It is not an annual percentage rate.
Size or moveEquity is a size. A dividend is a move. Check which one the question is asking for.
Find the state firstCompare VT with FS, then with FS + FJ, before you work anything out.
The adding-up checkSenior plus junior plus equity must equal exactly VT. That check alone kills most wrong options.
The equity floorEquity can never be negative. It stops at zero, because the owners have limited liability: they can lose what they put in, and no more.
r against gA growing perpetuity only works when r is bigger than g. If it is not, the value cannot be found this way.
Next year's paymentThe sheet writes the top of the growing perpetuity formula as CF. It means next period's payment, not the payment made today.
TimingA payment at the end of year 1 is discounted once. It is not left alone.
Where the annuity landsThe annuity formula gives a value one period before the first payment. A delayed annuity needs a second discounting after that.
Counting nn is the number of payments, never the number of years since today.
Coupon rateThe coupon rate is not the yield. The coupon is fixed cash on the par value. The yield is what the market charges today.
One date at a timeYou cannot add cash flows from different dates. Bring every piece to the same date first, then add.
When they disagreeIf NPV and IRR point at different projects, NPV is the answer, because NPV is measured in money.
The page 1 headingOn the sheet, "The value of a single cashflow" sits above the annuity table. Trust the row titles. "C per Period" means a repeating payment; "C Invested" or "C to Be Received" means one payment. "Invested" or "to Be Received" means one payment.
Letters change costumeAcross the sheet, C = A = CF for the payment, t = n = N for the periods, r = R for the rate. Same formulas, different costumes.
A(1 + g) in frontOn the growing annuity the sheet's front factor is A(1 + g), and that product is the first payment. If the question gives the first payment, that number goes in front as it is. Do not grow it again.

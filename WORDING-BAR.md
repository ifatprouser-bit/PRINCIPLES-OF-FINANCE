# WORDING-BAR — the English level for everything in this pack

Every agent doing a wording pass reads this file first, in full. You load no skills. Everything is
here.

Written 23 September 2026, after Ifat listened to the first audio strip and said the English was
too high. Her words: *"I am not a native English speaker. I have good English, but 'rim' and all of
the other are very, very high English. A lot of people don't know English that way."*

This pack is shared with her Bar-Ilan IMBA class before exam season. They are asking for it. Most
of them read English as a second language. **"Very nice English, but too high" is a defect, not a
style preference.**

---

## 1. The bar

Write for an adult who reads English as a second language and is good at it, but does not have a
large English vocabulary.

**The mechanical test: if a word has a shorter everyday twin, use the twin.** No judgement needed.
Apply it.

**This applies to everything**: the audio scripts, the page text, headings, table cells, captions,
button labels, quiz questions, quiz answer explanations, the formula page. Every word.

**It applies to the audio scripts too.** The `cut-the-fluff` skill says the second-language bar is
for on-page text and that audio stays conversational. Ifat overruled that on 23 September, and she
is right: conversational is about rhythm, not vocabulary. A script keeps its contractions, its
back-and-forth and its stories, and drops its hard words.

---

## 2. What must NOT be simplified

**The subject's own words stay.** These are the words the exam uses, and a learner who never
learns them cannot read their own paper:

annuity · perpetuity · discounting · compounding · present value · future value · face value ·
par value · coupon · coupon rate · yield · premium · discount (as a bond type) · senior debt ·
junior debt · subordinated debt · equity · dividend · asset · liability · maturity · inflation ·
credit risk · NPV · IRR · holding period return · Absolute Priority Rule

**Explain each one the first time it appears**, on that page or in that strip, in everyday words,
in the same breath. Never replace it, never drop it.

Numbers, formulas, file names and identifiers also stay exactly as they are.

## 4. Sentences, not just words

- **One idea per sentence.** If a sentence has two commas and a "which", split it.
- **Under about 20 words.** Longer ones get split.
- **Active voice.** "The bank gets paid first", not "the bank is paid first by the firm".
- **No word you would not say out loud** to a friend over coffee.
- **No long dashes** in learner-facing copy. Use a full stop, a colon, commas, or the actual words.

---

## 5. What you must NOT do

- **Do not change any number.** Not one digit, in a page or a script.
- **Do not change the teaching.** You are lowering the reading level, not rewriting the lesson.
- **Do not delete an explanation** to make text shorter. This is a wording pass, not a cutting pass.
  A visual does not license cutting the prose that explains it.
- **Do not remove a domain term** from section 2, and do not remove its first-use explanation.
- **Do not touch** `assets/theme.css`, `assets/study.js`, any `verify/*.py`, or any file outside
  the ones your brief names.
- **Do not simplify an idea into something false.** If the only simple version you can find is
  wrong, keep the harder wording and say so in your report. A plain sentence that is untrue is the
  worst outcome here, and it passes every other check on this page.

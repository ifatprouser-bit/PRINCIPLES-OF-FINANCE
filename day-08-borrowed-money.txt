NOA: Two women open the same kind of food cart. Same cart, same street, same profit at the end of the year. One paid for her cart out of her savings. The other put in a small part and borrowed the rest. Whose own money worked harder? Take a moment.

TOM: The one who borrowed. Same profit out of far less of her own.

NOA: That sentence is the whole idea. The carts are equally good, the owners aren't equally placed, and nothing about the cart explains the difference.

TOM: So there are two questions here, not one.

NOA: There are. How well do the assets work? Profit against everything the firm owns, and that's return on assets. How well did the owners do? The same profit, against the owners' money only, and that's return on equity.

TOM: Same number on top, different thing underneath. Two delivery cars, then. Same fares all year, one driver owns her car with no loan on it, the other's still paying it off. Per shekel of her own, the second earns much more.

NOA: And two women baking at home on the same oven. One paid for hers in full, the other pays the shop a bit every month. Same cakes, same customers, and only one of them can say her own money came back several times over.

TOM: And that payment leaves her account whether anybody orders cake that month. That's the part the oven knows nothing about.

NOA: The quiet month. Sales fall and the repayment leaves anyway. Nobody rings to say it can wait.

TOM: So a bad month for the cart is a much worse month for the owner who borrowed. The loss lands on a small stake instead of a big one.

NOA: That's leverage, which just means using borrowed money, and it swings both ways, always. Which tells you how to read a firm with a proud return on equity.

TOM: Ask how much is only borrowing. That number can be high at a firm run no better than its neighbour.

NOA: And a firm with no debt shows the same number twice. The gap between the two is debt, and nothing else.

NOA: One honest thing. Your course describes both in words and never prints a formula. The pair I gave you is the standard one, profit over assets and profit over equity, and some books put operating profit on top instead. If your lecturer wrote a version on the board, his wins.

NOA: Take a breath and recall one thing. Same profit, different number underneath. Debt is the only reason the two differ, and it makes the owners' number swing further both ways.

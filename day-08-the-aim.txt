NOA: You are paid to run a company and your bonus hangs on one number. Which number would you want it to be? Take a moment and pick one.

TOM: Profit. This year's profit. That's what everyone watches.

NOA: Then see what you just handed yourself. You run a cafe, December is thin, the bonus is close. So you skip the service on the machine.

TOM: Profit up. And the machine dies in March, when it's somebody else's problem.

NOA: Next. Market share, sell more than anyone on the street. So you drop the price below what the goods cost you.

TOM: A line out the door, losing money on every sale. Winning and sinking at once.

NOA: Third. Keep costs as low as they go.

TOM: The cheapest firm is one that does nothing. Replace no equipment, train nobody, buy no stock.

NOA: And the one people reach for last. Survive. Take no risk, never fail.

TOM: The safest driver never leaves the driveway. She's also never arrived anywhere.

NOA: All four can be made to look good by damaging the firm. So the course settles on a different kind of number. Make the current value of the firm's shares as big as you can. Shareholder wealth.

TOM: Which sounds like one more item on the list, until I remember where a share price comes from.

NOA: Say it.

TOM: It's the present value of the dividends the firm is expected to pay. So every future year is already inside it. The timing too. And the risk.

NOA: Which is why you can't fatten it by starving next year. Next year is already in the price. Skip the service and the price falls while the profit rises.

TOM: So it isn't a fifth goal. It's the only one that counts what the other four ignore.

NOA: Then the course sets one more thing beside it, carefully. Sustainability, what the notes call the triple bottom line: the financial result, the ecological one, which means the planet, and the social one, which means people. It says plainly this isn't the same as only making value as big as possible.

TOM: But not its opposite either.

NOA: It says that too. A firm that harms its workers, its customers or the ground it stands on tends to pay later, in fines, in customers who stop coming, and in the price of its shares.

TOM: So later turns up in the price today, because the price is a view of all the years ahead.

NOA: Take a breath and recall one thing. The manager aims at the current value of the shares, because that one number already holds timing, risk and every year still to come.

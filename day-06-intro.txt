NOA: One question before anything else. A friend borrows money from you, and writes down on paper how much and when she'll pay you back. Could you sell that paper to somebody else?

NOA: Take a moment. Really decide.

TOM: I think I could. She owes it to whoever's holding the paper. Not to me personally.

NOA: Then you already know what a bond is. A loan, written down, turned into something that can change hands.

TOM: So the word was never hiding anything.

NOA: It hides nothing. It has new words, and those are what you're carrying today. Face value, also called par value: what the borrower hands back at the end. Coupon: the fixed payment along the way. Coupon rate: the printed percentage that sets the size of that payment. Yield: what a buyer today actually earns.

TOM: Those last two sound like the same number to me.

NOA: They are today's one real trap. Then premium and discount, for paper worth more, or less, than its face value. Zero coupon, paper that pays nothing until the end. Bills, notes and bonds, the government's three lengths. Two risks, inflation and credit. And one word for a lender who agrees to stand at the back of the line: subordinated.

TOM: That's a pile of new words. But if a bond is only a loan written down, the maths has to be maths I already own.

NOA: All of it. Every price today is a single payment, or a run of equal payments, or a stream that never ends. All things you already did this week.

NOA: Take a breath and hold one thing. A bond is a loan somebody can sell.

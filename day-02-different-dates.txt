NOA: Here's the question. A friend borrowed from you for her wedding. She can pay you back now, or pay you back in a year and add a little on top. How much extra would you want?

NOA: Take a moment. Pick an actual number in your head.

TOM: I picked one. And it was easy. Nobody ever taught me that.

NOA: That's the point. You already know waiting has a price. You've just never given the price a name.

TOM: So the extra isn't just being polite. It's rent on my money.

NOA: It is. And the name is the interest rate.

TOM: Then it's everywhere. My salary lands at the end of the month, not the start. My employer holds a month of my pay for free.

NOA: They do. And it never shows on the payslip.

TOM: One more. My landlord offers a discount if I pay the year up front. Same thing from his side. Money in his hand now beats the same money dripping in later, and the discount is what he pays me for it.

NOA: One idea, three settings. Now the big step. Because waiting has a price, money on two different dates behaves like money in two different currencies.

TOM: So the same amount in three years, and that amount today, are two different things.

NOA: They are. You'd never add dollars to shekels and write one number underneath.

TOM: You'd change one into the other first. At the exchange rate.

NOA: And here, the interest rate is the exchange rate.

TOM: So adding two payments from different years is the same mistake as adding two currencies.

NOA: The very same mistake. And three things hide inside that one rate. People want paying to wait. Prices rise, so later money buys less. And a promise can break.

TOM: One number carrying all three.

NOA: One number. Take a breath and recall one thing. You can't add money from two different dates. Move them to one date first.

REFERENCE · EVERY FORMULA IN THE PACK

Every formula, in one place

Keep this open beside you while you work. Read it quickly before the exam.

Every formula below is copied from the day pages of this pack, in the same form those pages teach it. The real exam hands you its own formula sheet, so nothing here has to be learned by heart. The line under each formula is the part worth reading twice. It says when you reach for that formula and not another one.

1 · Payoff at maturity: who gets what

Day 7. VT is the value of the firm's assets at maturity. FS is the face value of the senior debt. FJ is the face value of the junior debt.

Senior debt

Min(VT, FS)

Use it when the debt comes due and you are asked what the senior lender collects. The senior lender is paid first, and never more than the face value.

Junior debt, also called subordinated debt

0

Use this when VT < FS. The senior lender is not yet paid in full, so the junior lender gets nothing at all.

Min(VT − FS, FJ)

Use this once the senior lender is paid in full. The junior lender takes what is left, but never more than his own face value.

Equity

Max(VT − FS, 0)

Use it when senior debt is the only debt. The owners take what is left after the lender, and they can never be asked to pay in.

Max(VT − FS − FJ, 0)

Use it when there is junior debt as well. The owners are paid after all of it. See the last panel on this page for why both lines are the same rule.

Two checks on every payoff answer. The three payoffs must add up to exactly VT. Equity is never below zero.

2 · One cash flow

Day 2. One amount, one date.

Present value, coming back

PV = FV / (1 + r)N

Use it when one amount sits on a future date and you want its value today.

Future value, going forward

FV = PV × (1 + r)N

Use it when one amount sits today and you want what it grows to by date N.

The rate, when both ends are known

r = (FV / PV)1/N − 1

Use it when the question gives you both amounts and the number of years, and asks for the rate of return.

The mock pages write these two with different letters: PV = CFt / (1 + r)t for the first and FV = CF0 × (1 + r)n for the second. Only the letter for the amount changes. The formulas are the same ones.

3 · Many cash flows that end

Days 3 and 4. A is the payment. n is how many payments there are.

Present value of an annuity

PV = A × [ 1 − 1 / (1 + r)n ] / r

Use it when the same amount arrives every period, the gaps are equal, the count is fixed, and you want one value today.

Future value of an annuity

FV = A × [ (1 + r)n − 1 ] / r

Use it when the same amount is paid in every period and you want the pile on the day of the last payment. Saving plans and pension questions.

Present value of a growing annuity

PV = CF1 × [ 1 − ((1 + g) / (1 + r))n ] / (r − g)

Use it when the payment grows at a steady rate g and the stream still has a last payment. Look for an end date: a license period, a lease length, a remaining life.

Growing annuity, the case where r equals g

PV = n × A

Use it when r and g are the same number, so the formula above would divide by zero. A is the current cash flow, the one before the first growth. Day 4 takes this from the course notes.

4 · Cash flows that never end

Day 4. Nothing in the question stops the stream.

Present value of a perpetuity

PV = A / r

Use it when the same amount arrives every period and there is no end date and no growth. One division, nothing else.

Present value of a growing perpetuity

PV = CF1 / (r − g)

Use it when the payment grows at a steady rate for ever. The top line is next period's cash flow, not the payment made now. It needs r > g.

5 · Stocks

Day 8. P is a price. D is a dividend. The number below the letter is the date.

Holding period return

r = (P1 − P0 + D1) / P0

Use it when you are told what you paid, what the share is worth one period later, and the dividend you collected, and you are asked what you earned.

Dividend model, no growth

P0 = D / r

Use it when the dividend is the same amount every year for ever. This is the perpetuity formula under a different name. Preferred shares behave this way.

Dividend model, constant growth

P0 = D1 / (r − g)

Use it when the dividend grows at a steady rate for ever. This is the growing perpetuity, so it also needs r bigger than g.

Next year's dividend, from the one just paid

D1 = D0 × (1 + g)

Use it first whenever the question gives you the dividend just paid. The model above wants next year's dividend on top, never the one already in your hand.

6 · Projects

Days 5 and 9. A list of dated amounts, and a decision to make.

Present value of a stream

PV = CF1/(1+r)1 + CF2/(1+r)2 + … + CFN/(1+r)N

Use it when the amounts are different from year to year, so no single shape covers them. Value each piece on its own date and add. A run of equal amounts inside the list can still be done with an annuity formula.

Net present value

NPV = Σ CFi / (1+r)i − I0

Use it when a project costs money today and pays later, and you must say yes or no. I0 is the cost today. Take the project above zero, reject it below zero.

Internal rate of return

The IRR is not a formula on the sheet. It is the discount rate that makes NPV exactly zero, and day 9 defines it in those words. Use it when the question wants the answer as a percent instead of in money. Take the project when IRR is above the required return r.

In the one case the exam can ask you to solve by hand, one amount out today and one amount in at date N, the IRR is the single cash flow rate formula from group 2: r = (FV / PV)1/N − 1. With more than one inflow you compare the rates you are given instead, because the equation cannot be turned around by hand.

7 · Bonds

Day 6. A bond has no formula of its own. It is priced from the pieces above.

The coupon payment

Coupon rate × par value

Work this out first, before any discounting. The coupon rate only fixes the size of the payment. It is never the rate you discount at. You discount at the yield.

Which pieces each bond needs
BondWhat it paysFormula to use
Zero couponNothing until maturity, then the par value onceOne cash flow, group 2
Coupon bondThe same coupon every year, plus the par value at the endAnnuity for the coupons, group 3, plus one cash flow for the par value, group 2. Add the two answers
Perpetuity bondThe same coupon every year for ever, and the principal never comes backPerpetuity, group 4
Price and yield move in opposite directions. The coupon never moves, so the price has to do all the moving. Coupon above yield gives a premium price, above par. Coupon below yield gives a discount price, below par. Coupon equal to yield gives exactly par.

8 · Used in the pack, not printed on the exam formula sheet

These two are taught in the pack but do not appear on the sheet the professor gives you. Know them.

Return on assets and return on equity

ROA = net income / total assets
ROE = net income / equity

Use them when a question compares two firms, or asks what borrowing did to a firm's profit. Same profit on top, a different number on the bottom. Debt is the only reason the two numbers are not equal.

Day 8 makes an honest note about these, and it is repeated here. The class notes in this course describe ROA and ROE in words but do not print a formula for either. The two lines above are the standard course book ones. Some books use operating profit instead of net income in ROA. If your lecturer wrote one version on the board, that version wins.

Nominal rate and real rate

R = real rate + expected inflation

Use it when a question gives a rate and an inflation figure and asks what you really earned. Take the inflation away from the stated rate to get the real rate.

Which one do I reach for

Read the question for two things only: does the amount repeat, and does it stop.

What makes a right formula give a wrong answer

Every row below is a mark this course actually takes off people.

CheckWhat goes wrong if you skip it
r must be bigger than gA growing perpetuity with r at or below g has no value you can work out. The formula still hands you a number, and that number tells you nothing, or it is negative.
Where the annuity answer is datedAn annuity formula gives a value one period before the first payment. If the first payment is not at the end of period 1, that answer sits in the future and needs a second discounting move back to today.
Where the FV answer is datedThe future value of an annuity is dated on the day of the last payment. The last payment earns no interest. Give it a year of interest and your answer is too big, by (1 + r) times.
Count the years from todayA payment at the end of year 1 is divided by (1 + r) once. It is not left alone. This one error moves every later number.
The payoffs add to VTSenior plus junior plus equity must come to exactly VT. If they do not, one of the three is wrong, and it is usually the junior one.
Equity stops at zeroOwners can lose everything they put in and no more. A negative equity payoff is always a wrong answer.
Senior before juniorThe junior lender sees nothing until the senior lender is paid in full. Paying them in the wrong order is the classic trap on this topic.
APR means Absolute Priority RuleIn this course APR is the rule that sets who gets paid first. It never means annual percentage rate.
Next period's cash flow on topA growing perpetuity and the constant growth dividend model both want next period's amount. If the question gives you the one just paid, grow it by (1 + g) first.
Discount at the yield, not the couponThe coupon rate only sets the size of the payment. Discounting at the coupon rate always gives you par back, which is the wrong answer for every bond except one priced at par.
Never add across datesTwo amounts on two different dates cannot be added until both sit on the same date. Draw the timeline before any formula.

The one place the sources disagree

The formula sheet writes equity as Max(VT − FS, 0) and the class slides write it as Max(VT − FS − FJ, 0). This pack treats them as one rule seen in two cases. The owners are paid last, so equity gets what is left after all the debt. With senior debt only, all the debt is FS. With junior debt as well, all the debt is FS plus FJ. The check that settles which line you need is the same in both cases: your three payoffs must add up to exactly VT. If they do not, you used the wrong line.

Small changes in wording across the pack
Written one wayAnd another way
PV = A × [ 1 − 1 / (1 + r)n ] / rPV = A × (1 − (1 + r)−n) / r. The two are the same, because 1 / (1 + r)n is (1 + r)−n.
Growing annuity, the bracket multiplied first and the gap divided lastGrowing annuity, the gap divided first and the bracket multiplied last. Multiplying then dividing gives the same number as dividing then multiplying.
A for the repeating paymentC or CF for the same thing. Day 13 uses C where days 3 and 4 use A. The letter carries no meaning of its own.

None of these change an answer. They are listed so a different layout on the exam sheet does not stop you.

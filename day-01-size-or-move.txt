NOA: You walk up to a cash machine and take out a hundred. Are you richer than you were a minute ago?

NOA: Take a moment. It sounds like a silly question and it isn't.

TOM: No. Same money. It was in the account, now it's in my hand.

NOA: Say what changed and what did not.

TOM: Where it sits changed. How much there is did not.

NOA: Hold that sentence. It's the most expensive confusion in this course. The balance is a size. Taking money out is a move.

TOM: So equity is the balance, and a dividend is taking money out.

NOA: Yes. And here is what trips everyone. Read that the equity is some number, and it's easy to hear it as: the company decided to hand out that much.

TOM: Which is how it sounds. As if managers sat in a room and chose it, and could have chosen less.

NOA: Nobody chooses it. Equity is what the shareholders' claim is worth, and it's pure leftover. What the assets are worth, minus what the lenders are owed. Whatever still stands there is theirs. One number taken away from another. It happens to them.

TOM: So it's not a decision, it's a result. The dividend is the decision.

NOA: The board chooses that one, and only that one.

TOM: Smaller version. I keep cash in a jar on the shelf and cash in my wallet. Move some from the jar to the wallet and the wallet is heavier, the jar is lighter, and I've earned nothing.

NOA: And asked what you are worth, you'd count both.

TOM: Both. Always both.

NOA: Now the firm. A bakery has a cash box. The owners take cash out and put it in their pocket. That's the dividend. The bakery is worth less by exactly what left. The pocket is fuller by exactly the same.

TOM: So the equity falls, and the owners aren't one bit better off. Which means if a question says a dividend was paid, I shouldn't say the shareholders gained.

NOA: Their wealth did not move. Only its address changed.

NOA: Take a breath and recall one thing. Ask whether the question is about how big, or about how much moved. Equity is how big. A dividend is how much moved.

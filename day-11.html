Mock 2: the time value paper

Twenty two questions, 150 minutes, sit it in one go. Nothing is shown until you submit.

How to sit this paper

Read this once, then start the clock and do not stop it.

Questions22, all multiple choice
Time150 minutes, about 6.8 minutes a question
CalculatorScientific only. Nothing that prices a bond or an annuity for you
Formula sheetBelow. The real exam gives you one too, so nothing has to be learned by heart
FeedbackNone until you submit. No score, no answer, no hint
MissesSaved to your error log automatically when the paper closes
This paper puts most of its weight on time value of money on purpose. Eleven of the 22 questions are single cash flows, annuities, perpetuities and mixed streams, so a weak spot there will show up here clearly.

Press Start, then work down the page. Pick an answer for every question, even one you are unsure of. A blank counts the same as a wrong answer and it teaches you nothing. If the timer runs out the paper closes itself and marks what you have.

Keep paper beside you. Draw the timeline before you touch the calculator. Almost every mistake in this topic is a timing mistake, not an arithmetic one.

Formula sheet

The same sheet on every mock in this pack. It matches the one the professor gives you.

Payoff at maturity

Senior debtMin(V_T , F_S)
Junior debt0 if V_T < F_S, otherwise Min(V_T − F_S , F_J)
EquityMax(V_T − F_S , 0)

V_T is the value of the firm's assets at maturity. F_S is the face value of the senior debt. F_J is the face value of the junior debt. Equity is what is left after every debt is paid, so when there is junior debt as well, the amount left for equity is V_T − F_S − F_J, and it stops at zero.

A single cash flow

Present valuePV = CF_n / (1 + r)^n
Future valueFV = CF_0 × (1 + r)^n

Annuity, the same payment A every period for n periods

Present valuePV = A × [1 − (1 + r)^−n] / r
Future valueFV = A × [(1 + r)^n − 1] / r
Growing annuityPV = [C_1 / (r − g)] × [1 − ((1 + g) / (1 + r))^n]

Perpetuity, a payment with no end date

PlainPV = C / r
GrowingPV = C_1 / (r − g), and this needs r > g

Stocks and projects

Holding period returnr = (P1 − P0 + D1) / P0
Stream of cash flowsPV = sum of CF_i / (1 + r)^i
NPVNPV = sum of CF_i / (1 + r)^i − I0

The annuity and perpetuity formulas both give a value one period before the first payment. If the first payment is not at the end of period 1, the answer needs a second discounting move back to today.
